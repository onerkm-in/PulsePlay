## 1. The problem PulsePlay is answering

A large organisation already has BI tools deployed, already has data in a
warehouse, and increasingly has AI agents built by a separate team. What it does
not have is a single place where a business user can look at a dashboard and ask
"so what should I do about this?" and get a governed, evidence-backed answer.

PulsePlay is that pane of glass. It does not build the LLM, does not build the
agent, and does not replace the BI tool. It hosts the BI surface, and it puts an
AI assistant next to it that reasons about whatever the user is currently
looking at.

Two things vary independently, and that independence is the whole design:

- **Which BI tool the user is looking at** (Power BI today; others are stubs)
- **Which AI brain answers** (Genie, a Foundation Model, or deterministic DAX
  with no LLM at all)

Either can be swapped without touching the other.

## 2. The demo scenario in one paragraph

You are a supply-chain manager at a LATAM consumer-goods company. Eleven markets,
three years of history, the current year half-done. Sales are up about 5 percent
and the company gross margin is improving, so the headline looks fine. The
question the demo exists to answer is the one a headline cannot: **which markets
are actually different, and which problem should I act on first?**

## 3. The synthetic data: what it is

Entirely invented, generated by `scripts/synthetic_poc/cpg_reskin.py` into
Databricks at `workspace.databrickspractice`. It is business-shaped, not derived
from any real company, and it is regenerable at any time.

**As-of window:** 30-Jun-2026. Three years - 2024 full, 2025 full, 2026 January
to June year-to-date. Nothing lands after the as-of boundary, so current-YTD
versus prior-YTD is a fair comparison.

**Star schema.**

| Table | Rows | Grain |
|---|---|---|
| `tbl_pp_syn_dm_countries` | 20 | market dimension |
| `tbl_pp_syn_dm_plants` | 602 | plant dimension |
| `tbl_pp_syn_dm_sales_channel` | 11 | channel dimension |
| `tbl_pp_syn_fct_performance` | 336 | country x year x month |
| `tbl_pp_syn_fct_operations` | 849 | country x plant x year x month |
| `tbl_pp_syn_fct_ofr` | 182,921 | country x plant x channel x year x month x week |

Metric views (`mtr_pp_syn_ltm_sc_fct_*`) sit on top and expose the measures Genie
queries with `MEASURE()`.

**What is measured.** Commercial: net sales, COGS, forecast versus actual
quantity. Service: ordered quantity, order lines, delivered quantity, OTIF lines,
from which fill rate and OTIF derive. Operations and sustainability: units
produced, planned and defective, hours worked, recordable incidents, lost-time
injuries, GHG tCO2e, energy kWh.

**Trends deliberately built in**, so the demo has something to find:

- Volume grows about 6 percent year over year, with a plus or minus 8 percent
  seasonal curve
- COGS ratio falls about 1 point a year, so gross margin trends up
- Order fill rate improves about 0.8 points a year; OTIF about 1.0 points
- GHG falls about 5 percent a year and energy about 3 percent, independent of
  volume growth - a decarbonisation story that is not just "we made less"

**Two deliberate imperfections.** The market dimension holds 20 countries but the
facts reference 11, and there are two orphan foreign keys (`ZZ00`, `ZZ01`) with
no parent row. Both are intentional: they reproduce the referential-integrity
imperfections of real warehouse data so the joins and the AI have something
realistic to cope with. The orphans are kept deliberately small so they can never
lead a ranking.

**Honest note on realism.** This is a 336-row performance fact, not a billion-row
warehouse. It is sized for a demo that regenerates in under a minute. Query
performance here tells you nothing about performance at scale.

## 4. The story the data tells

This is the part that only started working on 2026-07-28. Before that, every
market carried roughly the same sales and the same margin, so every ranking was
noise dressed up as insight. Each market now has its own size and cost structure.

Half-year 2026 versus half-year 2025, live from the warehouse:

| Market | Net sales H1-2026 | Gross margin | YoY sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brazil | $469.23 MN | 52.46 % | +7.11 % |
| Mexico | $387.80 MN | 53.47 % | +6.16 % |
| Colombia | $212.55 MN | 55.68 % | +3.69 % |
| Chile | $183.23 MN | 58.79 % | **-1.24 %** |
| Peru | $161.84 MN | 55.28 % | +3.32 % |
| Venezuela | $120.18 MN | **50.31 %** | +6.02 % |
| Costa Rica | $91.28 MN | 58.17 % | +7.42 % |
| Paraguay | $81.39 MN | 56.55 % | +10.64 % |
| Bolivia | $68.87 MN | 53.12 % | +1.54 % |
| Uruguay | $61.51 MN | **59.80 %** | +6.12 % |
| El Salvador | $53.17 MN | 57.37 % | +4.10 % |

Company total: **$1,810.42 MN to $1,902.44 MN, up 5.08 percent**, with gross
margin improving from 53.65 percent to 54.61 percent.

The narrative a demo audience should reach on their own:

- **Brazil and Mexico carry the volume** - together over 45 percent of sales -
  but they earn the thinnest margins, because they are competitive markets that
  import their inputs.
- **Uruguay and Chile earn the best margins** despite being small, on a premium
  mix and local manufacture.
- **Venezuela is the margin problem** at 50.31 percent, nine and a half points
  below Uruguay, and it is growing - so the problem is getting bigger.
- **Chile is the only market actually shrinking**, at -1.24 percent, and it is
  the fourth largest. That is the finding the headline hides: the company is up
  5 percent while its best-margin large market declines.

That last line is the point of the whole demo. A single number said "up 5
percent". The decision was somewhere else entirely.

## 5. What should happen when you run it

**Where you land.** The app opens on **Decisions**. That is deliberate as of
2026-07-28: you arrive at the stack of decisions waiting on you, not at a
narrative you have to read first.

**Decisions.** A ranked list of governed decision cards. Each states the issue,
the WHY, the FIX, and an impact figure, with evidence you can expand. Actions are
persona-gated: a viewer can propose, an approver can approve, and anything
awaiting sign-off says so plainly.

**AI Insights.** A briefing assembled in stages so the first card paints fast.
As of today, every section shares one set of numbers: the lead stage's rows are
computed once in code into a metric frame, and later sections narrate those exact
strings rather than doing their own arithmetic. If two cards ever disagree about
the same figure again, that is a bug, and it has a name.

**Ask Pulse.** Free-form questions in natural language against the Genie space.
Expect real SQL to be generated and run. The margin question above is a good
first ask: "which market has the weakest gross margin in Jan-Jun 2026?" It should
answer Venezuela at 50.3 percent and Uruguay at 59.8 percent.

**Dashboard.** The embedded Power BI report over the same star schema, so the
numbers should reconcile with what the AI says. This is the apples-to-apples
check that makes the whole thing credible.

**What should NOT happen.** Nothing queries the warehouse or an LLM because you
opened a page or because a timer fired. Compute happens when you ask for it. If
you see spend you did not trigger, that is a bug worth reporting loudly.

## 6. What this proves, and what it does not

**Proven, and verified live on 2026-07-28:**

- Natural-language questions reach a real Databricks Genie space, generate real
  SQL against the real star schema, and return correct numbers
- A deterministic metric frame keeps every section of one briefing numerically
  consistent - checked live, section values matched the frame verbatim
- Power BI works as a real vendor-SDK integration with an event and command
  bridge, and reconciles with the Genie path
- A no-LLM path exists: Power BI semantic-model answers are deterministic DAX
  with zero model calls
- The governance floor holds: scope prefixes, persona gating, and audit lines

**Not proven, and worth stating plainly:**

- **Answer quality is not measured.** The test suites assert output SHAPE, not
  correctness. "All green" does not mean "the answers are right". There is no
  eval or hallucination harness yet.
- **Six of the ten connector code paths are unproven live** - Azure OpenAI chat
  and analytics, Bedrock RAG and direct, Supervisor-local, ResponsesAgent. They
  are code, not evidence.
- **Tableau, Qlik and Looker are iframe stubs.** They render a URL. There is no
  event bridge, no command bridge, no vendor SDK. Do not credit them as
  integrations in any demo.
- **Scale is untested.** See the note in section 3.
- **The AI can still be wrong.** The frame fixes arithmetic consistency; it does
  not make the model's prose true.

## 7. What to take next

**To make the demo land harder:**

1. Wire the decision cards to the story above, so the top card is the Chile
   decline rather than a generic threshold breach. The data now supports a real
   narrative and the decision content should use it.
2. Run the headed validation of the hosted app, and repoint the Dashboard embed
   at the SCM report. A browser that last used the app in May still holds
   configuration for the older Superstore demo, which is a different use case
   entirely and will not reconcile with anything the AI says.

**To make the product defensible:**

3. **An evaluation harness.** This is the biggest honest gap. Until answer
   quality is measured rather than asserted, every quality claim is an opinion.
4. **The server-side spend budget** (`docs/DEBT_REGISTER.md` D7). All four
   no-spend-without-intent gates are currently client-side, which is fine for a
   demo and not fine before any autonomous executor exists.
5. **Converge the number formatters** (D8). Four code formatters still disagree
   with each other and with the documented convention.

**To keep it maintainable:**

6. Follow the burn-down rule in `docs/DEBT_REGISTER.md`: every substantial
   session retires one item or shrinks `visual.tsx`. Addition without subtraction
   is how this codebase dies.
7. Put a name in the "Architecture owner" line of `docs/MAINTENANCE_PLAYBOOK.md`.
   Until a human owns it end to end, the bus factor is zero and every
   owner-flagged decision stays frozen.
